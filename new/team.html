Founder & Chief Filmanthropist

Bret Warshawsky

Bret Warshawsky is a polymath founder, speaker, and futurist working at the intersection of consciousness, storylivingry, evolutionary and spiritual philanthropy, mental health reform, and Filmanthropy. His body of work spans more than a decade of practical co-creation and systems-design across Europe, Asia, and the Americas, including direct collaboration with the late futurist Barbara Marx Hubbard, co-founding Symphonics with creative partner Andrea Harding, co-creating Synergy Hub 1.0 in Rotterdam with the Meesteren Foundation and United Earth, contributing a chapter to Lisa Nichols's Amazon best-seller Unbreakable Spirit, and more than three hundred published essays at The Syntony Times Substack.

Today he is the founder of Social Effects Inc., the New Jersey startup pioneering Filmanthropy, the practice of making philanthropy a featured, designed-in outcome of the filmmaking process itself. He is the co-visionary behind the Mental Health Reformation leading development of the AWARE App for first responders and serving as an AWARE New Jersey Representative.

Andrew Alan

Creative and Strategic Narrative Advisor

Andrew Alan is a network weaver, storytelling strategist, writer, director, producer, and editor working across moving images, sound, words, and ideas. Born and raised in Berkeley, California and based in Europe, Andrew brings a transatlantic creative perspective shaped by a decade of concepting and visioneering across documentary, narrative, and strategic communications work. He holds a Bachelor's degree in Film Production, Screenwriting, and Directing from New York University's Tisch School of the Arts. For Social Effects, Andrew brings the rare combination of someone who understands how stories get made and how they land in communities, the connective tissue between a production's creative intent and the real-world relationships that make Filmanthropy possible to execute.

One shared mission

The Foundation and the LLC.

Intended nonprofit parent

Social Effects Foundation

the intended nonprofit parent, stewarding the charitable mission and programs spanning the Filmanthropy Ledger, research, storytelling, public education, philanthropic support, practitioner development, and community benefit.

Proposed operating enterprise

Social Effects Inc

Bret's working name for a for-profit LLC subsidiary owned by the Foundation. This proposed operating enterprise would develop a Film Biz Recycling-style New Jersey operation, with material and prop reuse, recipient relationships, recovery/logistics, on-set services, workforce opportunities and measurable production/community outcomes.

Explore the proposed franchise →

Foundation-led mission work, public education, research, philanthropy and funded community programs can coexist with LLC-delivered fee-based production services, implementation, advisory work and paid training or workshops where appropriate. Teaching and doing are not rigidly assigned to opposite entities: both contribute to one shared practice, with documented outcomes returning to the Ledger, presentations and curriculum. Each activity's legal, charitable, contractual, licensing and financial responsibilities would be assigned explicitly as the structure is developed.

Beyond production surplus

A practice that reaches across the production.

Film Biz Recycling-style material recovery is a core capability, not the limit of Social Effects Inc. The proposed LLC would also deliver Filmanthropy by Design and Regenerative Filmmaking services directly to productions, studios, independent filmmakers and the communities hosting them. These build on the existing Social Effects website's four production offers: Discovery (a pre-production conversation about possible social benefits), Design (a practical written plan with costs, partners and intended outcomes), Liaison (on-the-ground coordination and implementation with crews, recipients and communities), and Story (responsible documentation, reporting and communication of what actually happened). Productions could commission one service or an integrated engagement, across materials, human participation, proceeds/causes and potential enduring assets. The method can inform budgeting, hiring, local procurement, set construction, community access and what remains after wrap, rather than beginning only when surplus is discarded.

Social Effects Inc would also offer optional place-based advisory and implementation services for New Jersey Film Ready communities and other towns hosting productions. Working with municipal liaisons, production teams, local businesses, schools, nonprofits and community members as appropriate, it could help design local benefits before, during and after filming: business opportunities, workforce pathways, mentorship and access, reuse and donations, cultural participation, and lasting community assets where feasible. This independent service would complement the New Jersey Motion Picture & Television Commission's Film Ready and NJ Take 2 programs; it does not imply Commission endorsement or any role in government certification.

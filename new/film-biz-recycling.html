Proposed New Jersey operation

Film Biz Recycling
Franchise.

A proposed operation for material recovery, creative reuse and a coordinated connection between productions and communities.

The proposed operating enterprise

Material recovery is a beginning.

We are exploring a New Jersey Film Biz Recycling franchise: recovering sets, props, wardrobe and equipment, and connecting them with people who can use them.

The proposed operation would combine reuse, logistics, on-set support and local workforce opportunities. Partnerships and operating arrangements are still being developed.

It would sit alongside our production services: Discovery, Design, Liaison and Story. These help productions plan and carry out giving across materials, skills, access and proceeds. Explore our offerings and the proposed Foundation/LLC structure.

The Practice: What We Are Building

The full operation, mapped.

The proposed operation connects productions with communities through three streams: materials, human participation and proceeds.

Material

A production generates surplus across every department: set construction and lumber, wardrobe, food and craft services, equipment, props and set dressing, furniture, vehicles, flowers. The practice is built to route all of it (matched to recipients, with pickup, transfer, and intake coordinated), so that what now goes to a dumpster or a storage unit reaches the schools, shelters, pantries, and community organizations that can use it.

Human

The presence of cast and crew is itself a gift: workshops, set visits, apprenticeships, mentorships, screenings. The practice is built to coordinate it (matching what a production can offer to the organizations that would be changed by it), so a production can say yes without adding to its own workload.

Proceeds & Cause

The oldest form of Filmanthropy: a share of a film's proceeds, a foundation, a cause carried by the production. The practice is built to help a production design this early and structure it well, drawing on the lineage the Ledger documents.

The Process

From wrap call to community: one coordinated pipeline.

01

Production Intake

Social Effects audits your surplus inventory in pre-production or at wrap. One call. No complexity on your end.

02

Match & Coordinate

We match each category, material and human, to recipient organizations across New Jersey.

03

Logistics & Transfer

We handle pickup, delivery, and intake coordination. Productions should not chase trucks. We do.

04

Documentation

Every item is tracked, every recipient confirmed, every workshop and visit logged alongside every pound of food and piece of wardrobe.

05

Filmanthropy Impact Report

Tax-ready, ESG-ready, PR-ready. We deliver your production's full community impact, material and human, quantified and clearly documented.

And, On the Horizon: Two Standing Networks

Recipient Network Membership

Nonprofits, schools, shelters, food banks, theaters, and community organizations join a growing network of partners. Productions find you; we coordinate intake.

Our Position in the Ecosystem

The state has a foundation. We are building the full infrastructure.

The New Jersey Motion Picture & Television Commission’s NJ Take 2 Upcycling Program already connects productions with local educational, arts and nonprofit organizations that can use surplus sets, wardrobe, building materials and props. It brings sustainability and community benefit together. We celebrate that work.

Our proposed reuse operation would add practical capacity to this growing field: material recovery, logistics, recipient relationships and documentation. We want to complement existing programs and the work of pioneers such as EcoSet and Earth Angel, with more ways for productions to contribute and communities to benefit.

New Jersey’s film growth is something to celebrate: more productions, creative work, jobs and opportunities for local businesses. We want to help that success grow, supporting the people already making it happen and exploring what more a production can make possible for its host community. Explore the Filmanthropy Ledger →

What Is Filmanthropy

We define filmanthropy intentionally and broadly as the love of film and the love of humanity weaving together for exponential social effects; everything a film, or the making of it, gives to the people and communities it touches: proceeds, materials, presence, or something permanent left behind. This of course includes the films themselves as artistic gifts.

We did not coin or initiate the term Filmanthropy. It was introduced around 2008 by Ted Leonsis, and has been carried since by festivals, foundations, and filmmakers across the field. We honor all of it.

Film has always given back. Sometimes it is a finished film that moves an audience to act, or a director who turns a film's profits into a foundation. Sometimes it is the making itself: the sets, wardrobe, equipment, food, and props a production generates, and the presence of remarkable people doing remarkable work in communities seeing it up close for the first time.

Explore the history of the word and the people and organizations who have nurtured this tradition.

At Social Effects NJ, we celebrate the full spectrum of giving through film. Our presentations, labs and services help people explore Filmanthropy by Design and Regenerative Filmmaking: planning generosity and community social impact into the making of a film.

We are excited about New Jersey’s growing film industry and the work already happening across productions, crews, towns, schools and nonprofits. We want to help that momentum grow, with more opportunities for local work, learning, reuse and lasting community benefit. Programs such as NJ Take 2 show what productions and communities can accomplish together.

The Filmanthropy Spectrum

Three connected ways to give.

Sustainability, community giving and lasting local benefits can work together. Productions, crews and organizations like EcoSet and Earth Angel have helped show what is possible. We celebrate that work and help more people put it into practice.

01 The Floor

Sustainable

Do less harm. Reduce, recycle, divert waste from the landfill. It lowers a production's footprint.

02 The Middle

Contributive

A production actively gives back. Surplus is rehomed into the communities where filming happens: food, wardrobe, sets, equipment. And more than materials: the time, the access, the human presence a production uniquely carries. These contributions matter, whether planned early or made possible along the way.

03 The Summit

Regenerative

The production creates something permanent, for the community and for the world. Sometimes a place: the skatepark a Netflix production left standing in Rajasthan after Skater Girl. Sometimes a capability: a training pipeline or mentorship structure that outlives the shoot. It leaves something that was not there before. That lasting benefit can grow from many kinds of giving.

A production can also give through its proceeds, a foundation, or a cause (the more traditional home of the word Filmanthropy), and it belongs here too.

The practice

Filmanthropy by Design and Regenerative Filmmaking

Filmanthropy by Design is that design. The form is open: for some productions the gift is surplus rehomed and human presence offered to the community that hosted the shoot; for others, a share of proceeds, a foundation, or a cause committed to before a frame is shot.

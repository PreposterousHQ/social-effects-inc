Longer essays by Bret Warshawsky on the people, histories and ideas expanding what filmmaking can give; from AI to Filmanthropy to Regenerative Filmmaking and more.

In brief

What their work makes possible.

The film itself is already a gift. This essay follows women who helped filmmaking give in further ways: Mary Pickford helped organize care for film workers; Eva Radke connected production surplus with people who could use it; Vinati and Manjari Makijany helped create a skatepark that remained after filming.

Their stories reveal the practical work behind generosity: building relationships, organizing resources and sustaining what comes next. They also invite us to recognize overlooked pioneers and ask what else the films we make, and the ways we make them, could give.

Beyond AI

When AI Can Make the Movie, Why Make One Together?

If AI can create compelling films, why gather people to make one together? The experience itself has value: shared attention, learning, trust and changes in the people taking part, even when a film is not a hit.

The essay carries that question into civic life. Filmanthropy by Design and Regenerative Filmmaking can make a production’s contribution to people and places part of why it is made and supported. If physical production becomes less common, its human experience and lasting community value could help shape new reasons, partnerships and ways to fund filmmaking.

Beyond AI

The Irreplaceable Art of Being Human

Making art changes the people who make it. This essay explores the experience of creating together: rehearsal, vulnerability, trust, discovery and the relationships formed around a shared undertaking. As AI expands what we can generate, Bret asks how the value of those experiences might become more visible, and how the tools could help people collaborate without making the finished output our only measure of the work.

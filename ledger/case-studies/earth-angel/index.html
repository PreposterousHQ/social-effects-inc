Earth Angel

The East Coast Pioneer

Brooklyn · Founded 2013, merged 2024

Why this matters

Emellie O'Brien walked onto a film set, saw the waste no one was accounting for, and did the rarest thing in this lineage: she invented the job that did not exist, then built the company around it, and became the East Coast's defining operator in a field she helped name. What makes her story unusual is its ending. Earth Angel did not collapse, and its founder was not quietly hired away. After more than a decade of proving the work, O'Brien folded it into something larger by choice, trading a beloved brand for a longer reach. Lasting a decade is harder than shining for a few years, and choosing your own ending is its own kind of achievement.

Emellie O'Brien graduated from NYU's Tisch film program and walked onto her first professional film set in 2011. What she saw there, the cases of single-use plastic water bottles, the dumpsters full of struck set at wrap, the food going straight from craft services to the garbage, became the rest of her career. She took her first sustainability role with Big Beach Films that same year, a job that mostly meant sixteen-hour days digging through set trash. The crew, watching her sort recycling from compost over the walkie, started calling her Earth Angel. In 2013 she turned the nickname into a company.

The engagement that made the company's reputation came in 2014. Earth Angel's sustainability leadership on The Amazing Spider-Man 2 helped earn Sony Pictures recognition for the most eco-friendly blockbuster in the studio's history, and the production became the first to carry the Sony Pictures a Greener World end credit. Doug Belgrad, then president of Columbia Pictures, said the team's practices saved more than four hundred thousand dollars. The work was specific and measurable: nearly fifty tons of material kept for donation or reuse, a hundred ninety thousand disposable water bottles avoided, a fifty-two percent landfill diversion rate, thousands of meals donated, and tree planting in a Sandy-damaged stretch of New York's East River Park.

Darren Aronofsky worked with Earth Angel on Noah the same year and put it in a filmmaker's terms: 'When we did Noah, we knew we were making a film about the first steward of the earth, so we wanted to be good stewards ourselves. There's so much waste on film sets, and because of groups like Earth Angel, we were able to change that a little bit.' He came back to work with her on The Whale in 2022, where the production diverted eighty-two percent of its waste and gifted its stage flats to a local performing-arts academy.

In May 2014, one year into Earth Angel's existence, Eva Radke of Film Biz Recycling presented O'Brien with the Founder's Award for an Individual Displaying Utter Greatness, at the first Golden Dumpster Awards in Brooklyn. Film Biz Recycling's own Brooklyn warehouse would close in less than twelve months, though the work itself would move and carry on elsewhere. The torch was moving between two Brooklyn operators, visibly, in public, in front of the people who had built this field. O'Brien had in fact discovered Film Biz Recycling a few years earlier, while researching a green initiative for Focus Features, the older operation teaching the newer one before either had a name for what they shared.

On May 29, 2024, after eleven years and more than a hundred eighty productions, Earth Angel was acquired by TheGreenShot, a Brussels-based company, founded 2021, that folds financial, operational, and environmental tracking for productions into a single platform. The standalone Earth Angel brand was retired. The site that once booked its work now reads, simply, that after an incredible journey to make productions more sustainable, Earth Angel has closed. O'Brien continues inside the merged group as its Chief Impact Manager. The work she built goes on. The name does not, and she chose that trade herself.

"I knew what a powerful medium this was and really wanted to help leverage it toward educating folks on these important issues. It wasn't until I got onto my first professional film set where my eyes were really opened to the amount of resource inefficiency that was happening."

Emellie O'Brien, Earth Angel founder, Deadline, May 2024
